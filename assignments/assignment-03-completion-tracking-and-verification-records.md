## 1. Assignment Overview

The Northstar Potash Processing Facility project is progressing from equipment installation toward Mechanical Completion and Pre-commissioning.

The Construction Manager has requested that Systems Completion establish a preliminary Completion Tracking Register to monitor equipment completion, inspection records, outstanding work, and readiness for system handover.

The Equipment Register from Assignment 02 will serve as the starting point.

As Systems Completion Coordinator, I am responsible for tracking completion progress, maintaining verification records, identifying outstanding documentation, monitoring punch items, and reporting completion status.

I am not responsible for performing engineering inspections, conducting equipment tests, or certifying that equipment is complete. Those activities are performed by the responsible construction contractors, discipline representatives, quality personnel, commissioning teams, and authorized signatories.

## 2. Assignment Objectives

The objective is to understand how individual equipment items progress through the project completion process and how their completion is demonstrated through documented evidence.

By completing this assignment, I should be able to:

- Understand the purpose of Mechanical Completion.
- Understand the difference between Mechanical Completion and Pre-commissioning.
- Identify the inspection and test records required for equipment completion.
- Track equipment completion status.
- Record outstanding punch items.
- Identify missing or incomplete verification documentation.
- Link equipment tags to their corresponding completion records.
- Monitor completion progress at equipment, subsystem, and system levels.
- Produce a basic completion status report.

The main question this assignment must answer is:

> For each equipment item, what work and verification activities must be completed, what evidence demonstrates completion, what remains outstanding, and who is responsible for closing the gaps?

## 4. Completion Phase Definitions

Use the following definitions throughout the assignment.

### 4.1 Mechanical Completion (MC)

Mechanical Completion confirms that the equipment or defined scope has been installed in accordance with approved project requirements and is ready to proceed to the next completion phase, subject to applicable acceptance criteria.

Typical verification activities may include:

- Equipment installation inspection.
- Foundation and mounting inspection.
- Equipment alignment verification.
- Bolt and fastening inspection.
- Lubrication checks.
- Piping connection inspection.
- Electrical installation inspection.
- Instrument installation inspection.
- Completion of applicable construction inspection records.

The applicable requirements depend on the equipment type and approved project procedures.

### 4.2 Pre-commissioning

Pre-commissioning consists of checks and tests performed after Mechanical Completion to establish that equipment and systems are ready for commissioning.

Examples include:

- Motor insulation resistance testing.
- Electrical continuity testing.
- Motor rotation checks.
- Instrument calibration.
- Instrument loop checks.
- Equipment no-load testing.
- Pump rotation and functional checks.
- Conveyor functional checks.
- Piping flushing and cleaning.
- Pressure testing, where applicable.

Not every activity applies to every equipment item.

The responsible discipline and commissioning teams determine the applicable checks and acceptance criteria.

### 4.3 Commissioning

Commissioning verifies that equipment and systems operate correctly and perform their intended functions under the approved commissioning procedures.

Activities may include:

- Functional testing.
- Interlock testing.
- Control system testing.
- Equipment operation testing.
- Integrated system testing.
- Verification of operating sequences.

### 4.4 Handover

Handover is the formal transfer of a defined system or scope to the receiving organization.

Typical handover documentation includes:

- Mechanical Completion certificates.
- Approved inspection and test records.
- Pre-commissioning records.
- Commissioning records, where applicable.
- Punch list records.
- Approved drawings and relevant as-built documentation.
- Equipment manuals and operating documentation.
- Outstanding item registers and agreed handover conditions.

The exact requirements must follow the project handover procedure.

## 6. Required Deliverable 1 — Completion Tracking Register

Create a new worksheet named:

**Completion Tracking Register**

Use the following columns:

| Column | Description |
|---|---|
| Equipment Tag | Equipment identifier |
| Equipment Description | Equipment description |
| System Number | Parent system identifier |
| Subsystem Number | Parent subsystem identifier |
| Installation Status | Installation progress |
| MC Inspection Record | Applicable Mechanical Completion inspection record |
| MC Status | Mechanical Completion status |
| Pre-commissioning Status | Pre-commissioning progress |
| Commissioning Status | Commissioning progress |
| Punch Items | Number or reference of outstanding punch items |
| Overall Completion Status | Current completion status |
| Responsible Contractor | Contractor responsible for the applicable work |
| Remarks / Action Required | Outstanding actions, missing evidence, or clarification |

### 6.1 Suggested Status Definitions

Use the following status definitions:

| Status | Definition |
|---|---|
| Not Started | Activity has not commenced. |
| In Progress | Activity has commenced but is not complete. |
| Pending Verification | Work is reported complete but supporting evidence or verification remains outstanding. |
| Complete | Applicable requirements have been satisfied and accepted by authorized personnel. |
| On Hold | Progress is suspended pending resolution of an issue. |
| Not Applicable | Activity does not apply to the equipment item, subject to confirmation. |

Do not use Complete simply because a contractor reports that work is finished.

## 7. Required Deliverable 2 — Inspection and Test Record Register

Create a second worksheet named:

**ITR Register**

The purpose of this register is to track inspection and test records associated with individual equipment items.

Use the following columns:

| Column | Description |
|---|---|
| ITR Number | Unique inspection or test record identifier |
| Equipment Tag | Associated equipment tag |
| Inspection / Test Description | Activity being verified |
| Completion Phase | Mechanical Completion, Pre-commissioning, or Commissioning |
| Responsible Discipline | Mechanical, Electrical, Instrumentation, etc. |
| Responsible Contractor | Organization performing the work |
| Record Status | Current status of the record |
| Inspection Date | Date of inspection or test |
| Verified By | Authorized person responsible for verification |
| Remarks | Outstanding information or required action |

Use the supplied ITR references where available.

Do not invent additional approved ITR numbers. Where a required record is missing, identify it as missing or pending issuance.

## 8. Required Deliverable 3 — Punch List Register

Create a third worksheet named:

**Punch List Register**

Punch items are outstanding deficiencies, incomplete work, or documentation gaps identified during inspections, testing, or completion reviews.

Use the following columns:

| Column | Description |
|---|---|
| Punch Item ID | Unique punch item identifier |
| Equipment Tag | Associated equipment tag |
| Description | Description of outstanding work or deficiency |
| Punch Category | Classification of the punch item |
| Responsible Contractor | Contractor responsible for resolution |
| Date Raised | Date the issue was identified |
| Target Closure Date | Agreed target closure date |
| Punch Status | Current status |
| Verification Required | Evidence or inspection needed to confirm closure |
| Remarks | Additional information |

For this exercise, use these punch categories:

- Mechanical.
- Electrical.
- Instrumentation and Controls.
- Civil / Structural.
- Documentation.
- Safety.

Do not assume every outstanding issue is automatically a punch item. The project punch management procedure determines how items are classified and controlled.

## 9. Required Tasks

### Task 1 — Establish Equipment Completion Status

Using the supplied progress information:

- Populate the Completion Tracking Register for all eight equipment items.
- Record installation status.
- Record available inspection references.
- Determine whether MC can be considered complete, pending verification, or incomplete.
- Record pre-commissioning and commissioning progress.
- Identify missing information and outstanding actions.

### Task 2 — Establish the ITR Register

- Record the supplied inspection record references.
- Link each record to its equipment tag.
- Identify incomplete records.
- Identify equipment items for which inspection records have not been issued.
- Distinguish between a completed inspection record and an accepted completion requirement.

### Task 3 — Establish the Punch List Register

Identify the outstanding issues that require tracking.

For each issue:

- Assign a unique punch item identifier for this training exercise.
- Identify the affected equipment tag.
- Describe the outstanding work or documentation.
- Identify the responsible contractor where known.
- Identify what evidence or verification is needed to close the item.

### Task 4 — Determine Overall Completion Status

Establish a reasonable overall status for each equipment item based on the supplied information.

Do not declare equipment ready for handover without the required completion evidence and acceptance.

### Task 5 — Prepare a Completion Summary

Prepare a short written summary answering:

1. How many equipment items are installed?
2. How many have completed MC inspection records?
3. How many have incomplete or missing MC records?
4. Which equipment items have outstanding pre-commissioning activities?
5. What are the main completion risks?
6. What actions should the Systems Completion Coordinator prioritize?

## 11. Important Coordinator Principles

Remember the following throughout the assignment:

1. **Installed does not mean mechanically complete.** Installation must be inspected and accepted against applicable requirements.

2. **Mechanical Completion does not mean pre-commissioning is complete.** Each phase has its own activities, evidence, and acceptance criteria.

3. **A completed check sheet is not automatically an accepted check sheet.** Required verification and authorization must be in place.

4. **Missing documentation is a completion issue.** Record it, assign responsibility, and follow up.

5. **Punch items must be tracked to verified closure.** Do not simply change their status to closed because a contractor reports that work is finished.

6. **Completion status must be evidence-based.** Every status reported should be supported by appropriate records.

7. **The coordinator manages the process, not the technical inspection.** Responsible disciplines and authorized personnel perform and certify the work.
